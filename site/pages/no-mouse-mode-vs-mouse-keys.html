Short answer

Mouse Keys is a free, built-in macOS feature: turn it on in System Settings > Accessibility > Pointer Control > Mouse Keys, then move the pointer with 7 8 9 U O J K L and click with I. It works with no mouse or trackpad at all, so it's the right first step when one breaks. Its limits show up with daily use: you can't type while it's on, its page lists no right-click key, and it can't click a button by name. No Mouse Mode is the paid alternative for people who want the keyboard to be their mouse every day.

How to turn on Mouse Keys on a Mac

  1. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then click Accessibility.
  2. Click Pointer Control and turn on Mouse Keys.
  3. Click the info button next to Mouse Keys and turn on Press the Option key five times to toggle Mouse Keys. Now you can switch it on and off without opening Settings.
  4. In the same options, set Initial delay and Maximum speed to taste.

No working pointer at all? Press Option-Command-F5 (or triple-press Touch ID) to open the Accessibility Shortcuts panel, which can turn Mouse Keys on or off. The path above is the same in Apple's guides for macOS Sequoia 15 and Tahoe 26.

Mouse Keys not working? Check these first

  • "My keyboard stopped typing." That's Mouse Keys doing its job. Apple says: "When Mouse Keys is on, you can't use the keyboard or numeric keypad to enter text." Turn it off to type.
  • It turned itself off (or on). If the Option-five-times option is set, pressing Option repeatedly while typing can toggle it by accident. Turn it back on the same way.
  • The arrow keys do nothing. Mouse Keys doesn't use them. On a laptop, use 7 8 9 U O J K L.
  • The trackpad stopped working. Check whether Ignore built-in trackpad when Mouse Keys is on is turned on in the Mouse Keys options.
  • The pointer is painfully slow. Raise Maximum speed and lower Initial delay.

If the real problem is a mouse or trackpad that stopped responding, our Mac trackpad not working guide walks through Apple's fixes, including turning Bluetooth on without a pointer.

Where Mouse Keys falls short

Mouse Keys is a good emergency tool and fine for the odd click. As a daily driver, its limits add up:

  • You can't type while it's on. Every time you want to fill in a field, you toggle it off, type, and toggle it on again.
  • No right-click key. Apple's Mouse Keys page lists none. For right-clicks you turn on Alternate pointer actions in the same settings pane, or use Voice Control. See how to right-click without a mouse.
  • Fixed keys. The options Apple lists are the Option toggle, ignoring the trackpad, initial delay and maximum speed. There's no setting to choose different movement keys.
  • No scrolling keys, middle click or back/forward on Apple's page.
  • Aiming is slow. To press a button across the screen, you steer the pointer all the way there. There are no labels and no grid.

macOS has other built-ins that help: Full Keyboard Access moves between controls with Tab, and Voice Control can label buttons by voice. Each fills one gap, but none is a complete keyboard mouse.

What No Mouse Mode does differently

No Mouse Mode starts where Mouse Keys stops:

  • Click buttons without aiming. Press ⇧⌘Space and every button, link, tab and menu in the front window, plus the menu bar and the Dock, gets a letter label. Type it and it's clicked. Or press ⇧⌘/ and type part of the button's name.
  • Movement that feels like a mouse. W A S D move the cursor smoothly and speed up as you hold. Hold Shift to go fast or Q for one-pixel precision.
  • Every button. Space clicks (twice to double-click, hold to drag), Return right-clicks, M middle-clicks, [ and ] go back and forward.
  • Scrolling. Arrow keys scroll in every direction, and scroll areas (⇧⌘J) number each pane so you scroll the right one.
  • Reach anywhere fast. E opens a 3×3 grid that zooms in with each press; two or three presses reach any spot, in any app.
  • Typing still works. It also uses letter keys while it's on, but it switches itself off when you click into a text box, can stay off in apps you list, and has an optional hold-to-use mode (hold Right Command or Right Option). Shortcuts with Command, Control or Option always reach your app.
  • Your keys, your way. Every key and shortcut can be remapped.

Which should you use?

  • Use Mouse Keys if your trackpad or mouse just died, if you need a few clicks until a replacement arrives, or if you don't want to install anything. It's free and it's already there.
  • Use No Mouse Mode if you want to work without a mouse every day, for comfort, RSI or speed, and you need to type, right-click and scroll without toggling things on and off.
