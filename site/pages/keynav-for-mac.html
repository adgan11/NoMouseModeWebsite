Short answer

keynav can't run on a Mac. Its README says it "only works on X11", and macOS apps don't use X11. The closest thing on macOS is a keyboard grid that narrows the screen down to the spot you want. No Mouse Mode's jump grid does it with a 3×3 grid on Q W E / A S D / Z X C, two or three presses to anywhere. Free options exist too: warpd's grid mode (not updated since 2023), Voice Control's "Show grid" and Mouse Keys, each with trade-offs below.

What keynav is, and why it stays on Linux

keynav's tagline is "retire your mouse": it controls the mouse with the keyboard. It's free and open source, with about 653 stars on GitHub, and its last commit was in August 2022.

The reason you can't brew-install it on a Mac is spelled out in its README: "This project (keynav) only works on X11." The FAQ goes further: keynav is totally dependent on X11, and for macOS it suggests AppleScript instead. It doesn't support Wayland either. X11 is the window system that many Linux desktops use; macOS draws its windows with its own system, so a port isn't a weekend job, it's a rewrite.

The good news is that the idea travels. Mac apps can't reuse keynav's code, but they can do the same kind of thing through macOS's own accessibility and pointer controls.

The idea you're probably after: narrow down, don't steer

Most "keyboard mouse" tools make you steer the pointer like a tiny car with arrow keys. Grid tools take a different route. You see the screen split into cells, press the key for the cell your target is in, the grid shrinks into that cell, and you press again. A few keystrokes later the pointer is on the spot. It's fast, it's the same number of keys whether the target is near or far, and it never needs to know what's on screen.

That last point matters on a Mac. Click-label tools (like Vimium-style labels for the whole Mac) depend on apps describing their buttons to macOS. A grid doesn't care, so it works in games, design canvases, remote desktops and anything else.

No Mouse Mode's jump grid

Press ⇧⌘M to turn No Mouse Mode on, then E. The screen with the pointer splits into a 3×3 grid labelled Q W E / A S D / Z X C, the same shape as those keys on your keyboard, so you don't read the labels so much as point with your fingers. Press a letter and the pointer jumps to the middle of that cell, then the grid zooms into it. Two or three presses reach anywhere.

  • Space clicks there, Return right-clicks.
  • Delete goes back a level if you picked the wrong cell.
  • Tab moves the grid to your next display; Esc closes it.
  • Afterwards you can fine-tune with W A S D (hold Q for one-pixel precision).

The grid is one part of a bigger app. When a target is a real button, ⇧⌘Space click labels are usually faster: type the letters on it and it's clicked. You also get type to click, scroll areas, saved spots (Shift+1–9 to save, 1–9 to jump) and every key can be remapped. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later on Apple silicon or Intel.

The free options

warpd: the closest in spirit, but quiet since 2023

warpd is "a modal keyboard-driven virtual pointer" for X11, Wayland (sway/wlroots) and macOS. Its grid mode (⌥⌘G, written A-M-g in its docs) lets you pick quadrants over and over with u/i/j/k, then m, , or . to click. If you used keynav on Linux and want something that feels like a Unix tool, it's the natural first try. The catch: its last commit was June 2023 and its last macOS release (v1.3.5) was November 2022, and it doesn't state a minimum macOS. Full details in warpd for Mac.

Voice Control's "Show grid"

Built into macOS (System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control). Say "Show grid" for a numbered grid over the screen, or "Show window grid" for just the active window. Say a number to refine it, and "Hide grid" when you're done. It's the same narrowing idea, and free, but you speak rather than type, which isn't for every room.

Mouse Keys

Also built in (System Settings > Accessibility > Pointer Control > Mouse Keys). It moves the pointer with 7 8 9 U O J K L (or the keypad) and clicks with I. There's no grid, so it's steering rather than narrowing, and Apple notes that while it's on you can't use the keyboard to enter text. It's fine for an emergency; see No Mouse Mode vs Mouse Keys for the long version.

Two more grid apps

  • Scoot (free, BSD-3, v1.3 March 2026): a grid mode on ⇧⌘K with equal cells across all screens, each with a key sequence. Its README says it runs on macOS 11 and 12; newer versions aren't stated.
  • Mouseless (closed source, 14-day trial, prices not published on its site): tap Left Command to show a grid, type a cell's two characters, then Space clicks its center or a sub-grid key picks a sub-cell.

Which should you pick?

  • You only need to click now and then: start with Voice Control or Mouse Keys. They're free and already on your Mac.
  • You want the Unix-tool feel and don't mind risk: try warpd, knowing it isn't being updated.
  • You want a grid plus click labels, scrolling and remappable keys in one maintained app: No Mouse Mode, $19.99 one time for 1 Mac as of October 2026 (pricing). There's no free trial, but the live demo on this page uses the same keys.
