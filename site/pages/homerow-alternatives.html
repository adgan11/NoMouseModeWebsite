Short answer

If you want Homerow-style click labels plus a full keyboard mouse, No Mouse Mode ($19.99 once for one Mac) is the closest match with the most extras. Shortcat and Wooshy are search-first: you type a button's name. Scoot is free and open source, with labels, a grid and a freestyle pointer. Mouseless is grid-first and runs on Mac, Windows and Linux. macOS's built-in tools cost nothing and are enough for light use, and Vimium covers web pages only.

Why look past Homerow?

Homerow is a solid, maintained app: it labels buttons so you can click by typing, and it has search and scrolling. As of October 2026 it's $49.99 once for all your personal Macs, needs macOS 13 or later, and is free to try with a reminder every 50 activations. (Our Homerow vs No Mouse Mode comparison goes deeper.)

People look for alternatives for a few common reasons: they want to spend less on a single Mac, they want to move the pointer and drag without a mouse too, they want something free or open source, or they need the same tool on Windows or Linux. Each option below answers one of those.

1. No Mouse Mode: labels plus a full keyboard mouse

No Mouse Mode is a menu bar app that turns the keyboard into a mouse. Like Homerow, ⇧⌘Space puts letter labels on every button, link, tab and menu, including the menu bar and the Dock. ⇧⌘/ lets you type part of a button's name instead, and ⇧⌘J numbers each scrollable pane.

Where it goes further is the pointer. Turn it on with ⇧⌘M and W A S D move the cursor smoothly (Shift for fast, Q for one-pixel precise), Space clicks or drags, Return right-clicks, and the arrow keys scroll. E opens a 3×3 jump grid that reaches any spot in two or three presses, and you can save spots and jump between displays. An optional on-device text recognition mode adds labels in apps that don't describe their buttons to macOS. A Practice window with nine lessons helps you learn it.

Trade-offs: no free trial (the site has interactive demos with the same keys instead), all sales are final, and it needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. It's $19.99 for one Mac, $49.99 for three. For four or more Macs, Homerow's single license costs less.

2. Shortcat: a command palette for clicking

Shortcat calls itself "the universal command palette for your Mac". Press ⌘⇧Space, type what you want to click ("ok" for an OK button), and it clicks. After two seconds (configurable) it also shows yellow labels with one- to three-letter shortcuts. It supports right-clicks, double-clicks and modifier clicks, plus window search, menu search and an emoji mode, and it works in Safari, Chrome, Firefox and most Electron apps.

Trade-offs: it's still a 0.x beta, last updated in July 2025, with a beta expiry moved to 2030. Its site doesn't state a price; it's a free download. It has no pointer-movement mode on its feature list. More in No Mouse Mode vs Shortcat.

3. Wooshy: search the whole screen

Wooshy's pitch is "Type. Return. Done." It searches the whole macOS interface, including the Dock, the menu bar and the status icons on the right, and clicks what you pick. It's a search-to-click tool; it doesn't document a pointer-movement mode.

Trade-offs: the free version is "full-featured, but takes some random naps". To keep it running all the time you subscribe for $3.88 a month or $46.28 a year; there's no one-time option listed. It needs macOS 14.6, though older builds are offered for Ventura and Monterey.

4. Scoot: free, open source, three modes

Scoot is a "keyboard-driven MacOS cursor actuator" that can move, click, drag and scroll. It has three modes: ⇧⌘J shows labels on buttons and links in the focused window, ⇧⌘K shows a grid of equal cells across your screens, and ⇧⌘L lets you nudge the cursor with familiar text-editing shortcuts. All shortcuts can be changed, and it installs with brew install --cask scoot.

Trade-offs: its README only says it runs on macOS 11 and 12, so check it on your version. It's free (BSD-3-Clause), has about 535 GitHub stars as of October 2026, and was updated in March 2026.

5. Mouseless: grid-first, on every desktop

Mouseless offers "lightning-fast mouse control with the keyboard" on macOS, Windows and Linux. Tap Left Command to show a grid, type a cell's two characters, then press Space to click its center or a sub-key for a finer spot. Tap Left Option for free mode, where I J K L move the pointer. Label mode, Homerow's main feature, is still in beta.

Trade-offs: its site offers "lifetime or subscription" but doesn't publish prices. There's a 14-day trial with no card or email, each license works on 5 machines, and there's a 25% academic discount. It was updated in October 2026.

6. macOS built-ins: free and already installed

Before buying anything, see whether Apple's tools are enough:

  • Mouse Keys (System Settings > Accessibility > Pointer Control) moves the pointer with keys like 7 8 9 U O J K L and clicks with I. Apple notes that while it's on, you can't use the keyboard to type text. See No Mouse Mode vs Mouse Keys.
  • Full Keyboard Access (Accessibility > Motor > Keyboard) lets you Tab through controls and press Space to select. Our Full Keyboard Access guide covers it.
  • Voice Control can label items: say "Show numbers", then "Click" and the number. It also has a numbered grid.

These are free and dependable. They're slower than click labels for everyday use, but for occasional use or a trackpad that has stopped working, they may be all you need.

7. Vimium: link hints, but only in the browser

Vimium is a free, open-source browser extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Press f and every link on the page gets a hint; type it to follow the link (F opens it in a new tab). It's actively maintained and excellent at what it does.

Trade-offs: it only works inside web pages. It can't reach Finder, System Settings, the menu bar, the Dock or other apps, and Safari isn't on its list. If you love Vimium and want the same idea everywhere, read Vimium for the whole Mac.

How to choose

  • You want to leave the mouse behind entirely: No Mouse Mode, for labels, pointer movement, dragging and a grid in one app.
  • You'd rather type names than letters: Shortcat or Wooshy. No Mouse Mode's type to click does this too.
  • You want free and open source: Scoot.
  • You switch between Mac, Windows and Linux: Mouseless.
  • You only need it now and then: the macOS built-ins.
  • You only care about the web: Vimium.
  • You're happy with labels and use many Macs: Homerow itself is still a fine choice.
