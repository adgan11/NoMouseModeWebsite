Short answer

macOS has this built in: turn on Mouse Keys in System Settings → Accessibility → Pointer Control, then 7 8 9 U O J K L move the pointer and I clicks. It's free and fine for a few clicks, but you can't type while it's on and it has no right-click key. If you move the pointer by keyboard every day, No Mouse Mode steers it with W A S D, adds fast and one-pixel modes, a jump grid and saved spots, and gets out of the way when you type.

Option 1: Mouse Keys (built in, free)

Turn it on

  1. Open the Apple menu → System Settings → Accessibility → Pointer Control.
  2. Switch on Mouse Keys.
  3. Optional: click the info button next to it for more settings (below).

Shortcut: ⌥⌘F5 (or triple-pressing Touch ID) opens the Accessibility Shortcuts panel, where you can turn Mouse Keys on or off.

So 8 is up, K is down, U is left and O is right, with 7, 9, J and L for the diagonals. I sits in the middle as the click.

Settings worth changing

  • Press the Option key five times to toggle Mouse Keys. Turn this on. You'll be switching it on and off a lot.
  • Initial delay and Maximum speed: how long before the pointer starts to speed up, and how fast it gets.
  • Ignore built-in trackpad when Mouse Keys is on: handy if a faulty trackpad is making the pointer jump.

Its limits

Apple is direct about the big one: "When Mouse Keys is on, you can't use the keyboard or numeric keypad to enter text." Every time you want to type a URL or a message, you toggle it off and back on.

Apple's Mouse Keys page also lists no right-click key. For right, double-click and drag via keyboard shortcuts you turn on Alternate pointer actions in the same Pointer Control pane. And there's no way to jump across the screen or to another display: you steer the whole way.

Jump instead of steering

Long trips are where steering gets tedious. Press E and a 3×3 grid labelled Q W E / A S D / Z X C (the same shape as those keys) covers the screen. Press a letter and the pointer jumps to that cell, then the grid zooms in. Two or three presses reach any point, then you fine-tune with W A S D. It works in any app.

For places you visit all the time, like a toolbar button in your editor, press ⇧ and a number to save the spot, and the number alone to jump back to it. ` returns the pointer to where it was before its last jump.

Often you don't need the pointer at all

If the target is a button, link, tab or menu, don't steer to it. Press ⇧⌘Space and type its label, or ⇧⌘/ and type its name. These work even with No Mouse Mode off. Labels rely on apps describing their buttons to macOS, which most do. The grid and W A S D work everywhere.

When is Mouse Keys enough?

  • Your trackpad broke and a new one arrives tomorrow. Mouse Keys plus Full Keyboard Access will get you there. See what to do when your trackpad isn't working.
  • You click a few times a day and mostly type in between. Toggling with Option five times is fine.
  • You have a numeric keypad and like using it as a joystick.

When you'll want more

  • You move the pointer and type in the same minute, all day.
  • You right-click, scroll or drag often.
  • You have more than one display.
  • You're going keyboard-only for comfort. See using a Mac keyboard-only for wrist pain.
