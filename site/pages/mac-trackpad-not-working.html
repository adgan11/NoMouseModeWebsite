Short answer

Don't panic: you can do this from the keyboard. If it's a Bluetooth mouse or trackpad, press ⌘Space, type Bluetooth File Exchange, press Return, then Return again to turn Bluetooth on. Then switch the device off and on, restart if Bluetooth won't come on, or plug it in by cable for a minute. To keep working meanwhile, press ⌥⌘F5 and turn on Mouse Keys, which moves the pointer from the keyboard. If it's the MacBook's own trackpad and nothing helps, contact Apple Support.

Step 1: Apple's quick fixes for a Bluetooth mouse or trackpad

These come from Apple's support article, If your Bluetooth mouse, keyboard or trackpad isn't working. Go in order and stop when it works.

  1. Turn Bluetooth on without a mouse. Press ⌘Space, type Bluetooth File Exchange and press Return. Press Return again to turn Bluetooth on.
  2. Turn the device off and on. The switch shows green when it's on.
  3. Check Bluetooth in System Settings. Make sure it's switched on.
  4. Restart if Bluetooth won't turn on.
  5. Connect by cable for one minute, then unplug it. This pairs the device and charges it at the same time. A flat battery is an easy thing to miss.
  6. Think about interference. Apple notes that wireless interference can cause drop-outs.

Step 2: If it's the MacBook's built-in trackpad

Apple's article above is about Bluetooth devices, so most of it doesn't apply to the trackpad built into a MacBook. A restart is still worth a try. If the trackpad is jumpy or clicking on its own, Mouse Keys has an option to ignore the built-in trackpad while it's on (more on that below), which can stop it getting in your way. Beyond that, a built-in trackpad that doesn't work is a job for Apple Support.

Step 3: Keep working with what's built in

Your Mac has three free tools that work without any pointer device. You may need only one of them.

Mouse Keys: move the pointer with the keyboard

Press ⌥⌘F5 to open the Accessibility Shortcuts panel and turn on Mouse Keys, or go to System Settings → Accessibility → Pointer Control → Mouse Keys. Then:

  • 7 8 9 U O J K L move the pointer (or the numeric keypad, if you have one).
  • I clicks (keypad 5). M presses and holds, . releases.

Two catches: while Mouse Keys is on, you can't type text with those keys, and Apple's Mouse Keys page lists no right-click key. Its options (the info button next to Mouse Keys) include "Press the Option key five times to toggle Mouse Keys", "Ignore built-in trackpad when Mouse Keys is on", and the pointer's speed. Our No Mouse Mode vs Mouse Keys page goes deeper.

Full Keyboard Access: Tab to buttons

Turn it on in System Settings → Accessibility → Motor → Keyboard. Then Tab moves a highlight between items and Space selects. It's great for dialogs and settings screens. Full details in our Full Keyboard Access guide.

Voice Control: click by speaking

Turn it on in System Settings → Accessibility → Voice Control. Say "Show numbers" to put a number on each item, then "Click" and the number. "Show grid" gives you a numbered grid for clicking anywhere, and "Hide numbers" or "Hide grid" removes them.

Keep working with No Mouse Mode

If you'd rather not juggle three accessibility features, No Mouse Mode turns the keyboard into a full mouse. Press ⇧⌘Space to label every button, link and menu (plus the menu bar and the Dock) and type a label to click it. Or press ⇧⌘M and glide the pointer with W A S D: Space clicks, Return right-clicks, hold Space to drag, and the arrow keys scroll. Shortcuts with Command, Control or Option still go straight to your apps.

Be realistic about two things:

  • You need to be able to install it. Setup normally means dragging the app into Applications, then switching on the Accessibility permission in System Settings. We haven't documented doing that with the keyboard alone, so we don't promise it. The easy way round it: borrow any wired mouse for a few minutes, or better, set it up now while your pointer still works.
  • It's paid, with no free trial. $19.99 one-time for 1 Mac, as of October 2026 (see pricing). You can try the same keys in the demo on this page first. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon or Intel. If you only need to get through a day or two, the free built-ins above may be plenty.

Once it's set up, it doesn't need a mouse or trackpad at all, and it doesn't fix hardware either: it just means a broken trackpad doesn't stop your work. New to it? Open Practice from the menu bar icon for nine short lessons, or press H while it's on to see every key. Questions are answered on the support page.

When to call Apple

Contact Apple Support if:

  • a Bluetooth mouse or trackpad still won't connect after the steps above, even after charging it by cable;
  • the built-in MacBook trackpad doesn't respond, or keeps clicking or moving on its own after a restart.

Until then, your keyboard has you covered. For the full picture of working without a pointer, read our guide to using a Mac without a mouse.
