Click labels · ⇧⌘Space

Click anything by typing two letters.

Press ⇧⌘Space in any app, even with No Mouse Mode off, and every button, link, tab and menu gets a small label. Type it and it's clicked. The labels for a window stay the same each time, so they turn into muscle memory.

Hold a key with the last letter for a different click: ⇧ right-click, ⌘ open in a new tab, ⌥ double-click, ⌃ just move the pointer there. Tab keeps the labels coming back for click after click.

Jump grid · E

Anywhere on screen in three keys.

Press E and the screen splits into nine cells, named after the keys under your left hand: QWE / ASD / ZXC. Press one and the pointer jumps to it as the grid zooms in. Two or three presses land on anything.

Space clicks there, Delete steps back, Tab moves to your next display. It works on anything you can see, even in apps where labels can’t reach: games, canvases, remote desktops.

Questions, answered.

How is it different from other keyboard-mouse apps?

Most do one thing: labels, or a grid, or arrow-key movement. No Mouse Mode does all of it in one small app — click labels, type to click, smooth W A S D movement, a jump grid, scroll areas, saved spots and screen jumps — with a built-in practice so you're fluent the same day. It's a one-time $19.99, not a subscription.

Do labels work in every app?

They work in apps that describe their buttons to macOS: Finder, Safari, Chrome, Mail, Notes, System Settings and most Mac apps and websites. Some apps don't (certain games, design canvases, remote desktops). For those, turn on Also read text on the screen in Settings → Clicking, and the jump grid and W A S D work everywhere.

Is reading the screen private?

Yes. It's off unless you turn it on, it uses Apple's text recognition on your Mac, and nothing it sees is saved or sent anywhere. macOS calls it Screen Recording, so it asks for that permission the first time.

Can I still type while No Mouse Mode is on?

The anywhere shortcuts (⇧⌘Space, ⇧⌘/, ⇧⌘J) don't need it on at all. While it's on, only the keys it uses are taken, any shortcut with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ goes straight to your app, and clicking into a text box turns it off so you can type. You can also list apps where it stays off.

How long does it take to learn?

A couple of minutes. Setup ends with a short hands-on try, the Practice window has nine lessons that tick themselves off, and H shows every key whenever you forget one.

Does it work with more than one display?

Yes. The pointer glides across, Tab jumps to the middle of the next display, the grid works on whichever screen you're on, and labels appear wherever the window is.

Can I change the keys?

Every key and every shortcut, in Settings → Keys and General. One button puts them all back.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma, 15 Sequoia and 26 Tahoe, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

How many Macs can I use it on?

The standard license covers 1 Mac for $19.99. Need more? There are 3-Mac ($49.99), 5-Mac ($79.99) and 20-Mac ($299.99) licenses, one key for all of them. Move a license to another Mac any time from Settings → License → Deactivate.
